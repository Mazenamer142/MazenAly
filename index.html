Finding 1

Revenue climbs while margin slips

Monthly revenue peaked at $884k in March 2023 and stayed well above 2022 levels. Monthly margin moved the other way: it was 30.8% in January 2022 and fell to a low of 25.3% in July 2023, recovering to 27.4% by September. Two charts, not one dual-axis chart, so neither scale can mislead.

Finding 2

It is a mix problem, not a price problem

Every product has one fixed price and one fixed cost in this data, so the blended margin can only move when the sales mix moves. Two products explain most of it. Colorbuds (53% margin) fell from 17.5% to 6.3% of revenue: −2.7 points. Magic Sand (12.5% margin) rose from 0.1% to 12.5% of revenue: −2.1 points. Magic Sand added $108k of profit, so it is a good product with a diluting margin, not a bad one.

Across all 35 products the contributions add up to −3.4 points, matching the actual change. The chart shows the 4 biggest drags and 4 biggest lifts.

Finding 3

Ranking by revenue points at the wrong products

Lego Bricks is the biggest product by revenue ($2.39M) but earns a 12.5% margin, so only 7.4% of profit. Colorbuds alone earns 20.8% of all profit, and the top 5 products earn 47.9%. By category, Toys are 35% of revenue but 27% of profit, while Electronics are 16% of revenue but 25% of profit. Electronics profit fell 39% year on year, which is the Colorbuds story again.

Finding 4

Airport stores earn more each. Downtown is just bigger.

Downtown makes 56% of profit only because it has 29 of the 50 stores. Per store, the 3 airport stores earn about $126k each, roughly 63% more than the other location types (about $77k). Two of them are the most profitable stores in the chain. Friday to Sunday is also 51% of all revenue.

Finding 5

Stock is running out where it costs the most

77 of 1,593 store-and-product pairs (4.8%) are out of stock. The most exposed product is Playfoam, a 64%-margin line that is out of stock in 13 stores. Ranking restocks by money at risk, not by count, puts it first.

Recommendations

What I would do next

  1. Investigate Colorbuds first.Units fell 53%, worth about $264k of profit. The data cannot say why, so check availability, supplier and competitor factors before assuming demand fell.
  2. Rank products by profit and margin, not revenue.Review supplier terms or pricing on the 12.5%-margin volume lines (Lego Bricks, Magic Sand). Pair Magic Sand with high-margin lines such as Barrel O' Slime (50%) and Etch A Sketch (48%).
  3. Restock by money at risk.Playfoam first, then Action Figure and Etch A Sketch.
  4. Test more airport-type locations.The per-store gap is large, but the data has no market information, so treat it as a hypothesis to test.
  5. Collect better data.Daily stock history, price and cost history, promotions, and customer or basket data would turn several estimates into measurements.

How much to trust it

Checks and limits

  • Every figure was calculated twice, in SQL and in pandas. The Python script stops with an error if they ever disagree.
  • The data is clean: no nulls, no duplicate sale IDs, no orphan store or product IDs. Cost and price were stored as text ("$9.99 ") and were converted; Mexico City was spelled two ways and was standardised.
  • Prices and costs never change in the data, so price effects cannot be separated from mix effects.
  • 157 of the 1,750 store-and-product pairs are missing from the inventory table (41 of them still sell). I flag them as a data gap, not as zero stock.
  • No customer, basket, promotion or competitor data, so there is no customer or pricing analysis.
